Warning Signs for Heating Repair

The sooner you notice these red flags, the easier and cheaper the problem is to resolve. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • The system starting and stopping every few minutes without warming the house
  • Cold air, or no air at all, when the thermostat calls for heat
  • An outdoor heat pump coil that stays iced over
  • Clicking and failed ignition attempts before the furnace gives up
  • Cold rooms in one part of the house while others are comfortable
  • Strange smells from the vents, especially rotten egg or burning
  • Higher heating bills with less comfort to show for them
  • A loud boom when the burners light, which points to delayed ignition

Why Hire a Professional for Heating Repair?

Combustion is what makes heating repair different from the rest of home comfort work. A late light-off booms unburned gas inside the heat exchanger and stresses the metal each time it happens. A cracked exchanger can leak combustion gases into the air your family breathes, and a flue that stops drafting pushes carbon monoxide into the living space instead of outdoors. None of these problems make themselves obvious to someone without training and instruments. Diagnosis has its own traps as well. Short cycling can point to a failing limit switch or to a furnace protecting itself from a clogged filter, and cleaning a fouled flame sensor without asking why simply sets up the next lockout.

Instruments and judgment are what our experienced technicians bring to both sides of that problem. We measure the flame signal instead of assuming it, inspect the heat exchanger, verify draft at the flue, and check temperature rise against the data plate so blower speed and burner output are matched. With heat pumps, the defrost board, sensors, and reversing valve are tested before anyone condemns a compressor, and we look at whether the backup strips have been running up your bill for weeks. Each repair we complete is proven safe and correct over a full heating cycle, which is the standard you should expect from anyone working on the equipment that keeps your family warm.

Heating Repair Prevention Tips

A little routine maintenance goes a long way toward avoiding a mid-season breakdown. Start with these practical measures our team relies on:

  • Test carbon monoxide detectors monthly and put one on every level of the home
  • Keep chlorine products, paints, and solvents away from the furnace, because their fumes corrode burners
  • Keep heat pump outdoor units clear of snow, leaves, and shrubs so defrost can do its job
  • Avoid crowding the furnace with storage, which can starve the burners of air
  • Change the filter monthly in heating season; starved airflow trips limit switches and cracks exchangers early
  • Schedule a professional tune-up every fall before the first cold snap finds the weak parts
  • Call about booming at startup or new rattles right away, while the fix is still inexpensive
